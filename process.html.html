Nothing connects the Manitoba and Nunavut processes. Manitoba licenses its own segment. Nunavut's statute reaches both, since ss.98 and 156–157 apply to the whole project and s.90(i) makes cumulative effects mandatory at screening. Coordinating machinery exists on both sides and has never been used here.

Reading the precedence columns

Position comes from the edges rather than from stages I invented, and the merged headers above say which stage each span belongs to. Two things show up that the stage view concealed.

  • The downstream permits are not really downstream. The only thing holding them is the screening gate at NuPPAA s.75, so by precedence they sit immediately after screening — earlier than the hearing, earlier than either final decision. A permit can issue before anyone has weighed the project as a whole.
  • Cabinet's designation sits in the first column with nothing before it. Nothing has to happen for that order to be made, and nothing compels it ever to be made.
